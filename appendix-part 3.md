# 2. Broader Sample

The Broader Sample contains **66 security companies** and was fixed before Action events were coded.

It is a **purposive comparison set**, not a statistically representative sample of the global cybersecurity market.

The sample is used to test whether patterns observed among the TOP-11 large security platforms also appear among a wider set of security companies.

The Broader Sample does not contain companies from the TOP-11.

Regional grouping is operational and is used only to describe the composition of the sample.

# 3. Action Taxonomy

Action events are classified using a two-level structure:

**Domain → Direction**

The taxonomy contains:

* **10 domains**
* **57 directions**

The taxonomy is an analytical framework developed for this research. Some domains intentionally combine capabilities that may appear as separate purchasing categories in the commercial security market.

Detailed classification rules are described in the [methodology](methodology.md).

# 6. Expo / Commercial Positioning

Expo / Positioning analysis uses publicly available:

* exhibitor lists;
* solution-provider lists;
* sponsor lists

from major cybersecurity industry events.

Expo presence is interpreted as evidence of **vendor-side commercial positioning**.

An increase in the number of vendors associated with a category indicates that more vendors are positioning themselves around that category.

It does **not** directly demonstrate:

* customer purchases;
* product adoption;
* revenue growth;
* market share.

Expo results are therefore treated as a separate signal rather than as a measure of market demand.

---

# 7. Patent Sources

Patent material is used **qualitatively**.

The principal patent databases used in the research are:

1. USPTO
2. WIPO
3. EPO Espacenet
4. Google Patents

Patent activity is used to identify technical themes and development directions that may sit behind broader commercial categories.

Patent counts are **not used to rank companies**.

Direct comparisons between company patent counts would be affected by differences in:

* corporate patent strategy;
* jurisdiction;
* filing practice;
* corporate structure;
* publication delays.

Patent data therefore supports interpretation but is not treated as a quantitative market-ranking signal.

---

# 8. Relationship Between the Data Layers

The research intentionally keeps the major evidence layers separate:

**Action**
→ observable commitment of vendor resources through completed acquisitions and GA product launches.

**Narrative**
→ recurring themes in forecasts and descriptions of the market's future.

**Conference Talks**
→ professional and research attention.

**Expo / Positioning**
→ commercial positioning by vendors.

**Patents**
→ evidence of technical development and research direction.

These signals are compared for **convergence and divergence**.

They are not combined into a single score because they measure different phenomena and operate on different time horizons.

For the complete rules governing inclusion, counting and interpretation, see:

**[Methodology — Security Forecast 2026–2028](methodology.md)**

---

# 9. What These Materials Do Not Measure

Neither this appendix nor the underlying study directly measures:

* customer adoption;
* product renewals;
* production deployment;
* product effectiveness;
* revenue attributable to individual capabilities;
* customer security spending;
* market share.

The findings therefore describe **vendor activity, professional attention and market positioning**, rather than direct customer demand.

# 10. Research Cutoff

The research cutoff for this version is:

**28 July 2026**

Events occurring after this date are not retrospectively incorporated into the dataset used for the published analysis.

This is particularly important for the forecasts in the article: future evaluation should compare subsequent developments with the dataset as it existed at the research cutoff rather than modify the original baseline.
